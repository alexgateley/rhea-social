Prohibition shut most Napa wineries. Many survivors sold altar wine.

From 1920 to 1933, selling wine was banned across America, with narrow exceptions. The Volstead Act left one door open: wine for religious services, made under permit and sold only to clergy. Beaulieu Vineyard had its permit before the ban began; by the winery's own account it grew production fourfold. By 1924 nearly three million gallons a year were withdrawn as sacramental wine; a 1925 church council report said legitimate demand "does not increase 800,000 gallons in two years." Counts of Napa's survivors differ: 12 by the S.F. Examiner's account, about 40 by the Napa Valley Register's.

The wineries that lasted had their second channel ready before the first one closed.
