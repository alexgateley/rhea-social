Burger King's franchisees sued Burger King over a $1 cheeseburger.

In October 2009 Burger King set $1 as the double cheeseburger's maximum price. Franchisees had voted against it more than once that summer, and in November their association, which said it represents more than 80 percent of U.S. franchised Burger King restaurants, sued in federal court in Miami. Franchisees said the sandwich cost them about $1.10 all-in, so each one lost about 10 cents. Burger King said the suit was without merit and that value deals drove traffic and profitable sales.

In 2010 the court sided with Burger King on its right to set a maximum price and later dismissed the case, finding no bad faith, as reported. In April 2011 the two sides settled, with a new policy for value menu decisions.
