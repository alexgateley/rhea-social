$81,000 of cabernet in barrels. Not one bottle for sale until 2028.

Example numbers: 20 barrels from the 2026 harvest, about 300 bottles each, 6,000 bottles. This fall: grapes at $9 a bottle and new French oak at about $4.50, so $81,000 goes out now. Then 18 months in oak with nothing to sell, and on accrual books the cost sits in inventory, not on the P&L. Bottling in spring 2028 adds $2.50 a bottle: $96,000 in. Sold at $60, 2,000 bottles a year, the cash comes back around summer 2029, before the room's own costs. Carry the $81,000 on a credit line at an example 8% and two years of interest is about $13,000.

That's how a winery looks profitable on paper and still runs short of cash. Plan the two years before you pick.
